6-MINUTE FIELD GUIDE

How to track pet-sitting income and expenses.

A repeatable record-keeping routine can make it easier to see what you booked, what clients paid, and which business costs you logged.

Pet-sitting work often happens in short visits across many households. If booking notes, payment messages, receipts, and mileage records live in different places, it can take time to reconstruct a month. A small routine and a consistent record can make the review simpler.

1. Record each booking when it is confirmed

Give each booking a date, client, pet, service description, and agreed price. If one client books several visits, create a separate row for each visit or clearly define a booking period. Consistent rows make it easier to total the month and find an individual service later.

Use the same names or IDs for the client and pet in each record. Keep the information needed to perform the service in a secure place. A spreadsheet is not a safe place for door codes, alarm codes, passwords, payment-card numbers, or detailed medical records.

2. Mark the visit and payment separately

A completed visit does not always mean the payment has arrived. Keep the service status and payment status as different fields. Record the actual amount received and calculate any balance still due from the agreed booking amount.

At month end, compare payment records with the payment processor or bank activity you use. Investigate differences instead of treating a formula total as proof that the money arrived.

3. Log business expenses consistently

Record each business expense with the transaction date, amount, category, vendor, and a short description. Keep the receipt or original documentation in the system you use for business records. Use categories that make sense to you and stay consistent from month to month.

If you record work travel, note the date and business purpose in a separate mileage log. A mileage field by itself does not calculate a tax deduction. Tax treatment depends on your situation, records, and applicable rules, so ask a qualified tax professional about your own filing.

4. Review the month, then save a copy

Choose a regular time to mark completed visits, update payments, log costs, and compare the totals with source records. Save a dated backup in a secure place. If you change a rate or service category, record when the change started so older bookings remain understandable.

5. Use a tool that matches the size of the business

A spreadsheet can work for a solo operator who wants an editable booking and payment log. It will not send reminders, share a live calendar, manage staff permissions, or collect client signatures. If you need those features, compare dedicated pet-sitting software rather than expecting a basic workbook to provide them.

The SitKit tracker concept is an Excel workbook with a booking log, monthly summary, client and pet lists, service and expense logs, and a rate-planning sheet. Its formulas help organize the information you enter; they do not replace your accounting system or source documents.

Record keeping is not tax advice. This guide is general organizational information. Keep the source records your business needs and consult a qualified professional about tax and legal requirements.
