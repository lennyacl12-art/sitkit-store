5-MINUTE FIELD GUIDE

What to keep in a pet-sitting client and pet record.

A clear record can help you find the practical details you need before a visit. Keep the record short, current, and stored with the level of security the information deserves.

Start with the minimum useful information

For a solo pet sitter or dog walker, a client list usually needs a name, a reliable way to contact the client, and a reference that links them to their pets and bookings. Add only the information your workflow actually uses.

Keep pet profiles separate from visit rows

A household may have more than one pet. A separate pet record makes it easier to keep names, species, size, and ordinary care preferences together without repeating those details in every booking. Link each pet to a stable client ID or name.

Keep operational notes current

When the client changes feeding, walking, or handoff instructions, update the record and note when it changed. Do not rely on an old note for a safety-critical detail. Confirm important instructions directly with the client through a channel you already trust.

Protect access and health information

Do not store home entry codes, alarm details, account passwords, payment-card data, or sensitive medical records in a basic spreadsheet. Keep access credentials in a secure access-management method. For care and emergency details, use a service or record system suitable for sensitive information and agree with the client how it will be handled.

Use a separate file for each business or client only when you can protect it appropriately. Restrict access, secure backups, and remove records you no longer need according to your written business policy and applicable requirements.

Use a consistent link between records

Give each client a simple ID and use it in booking and pet records. This reduces confusion when clients share similar names. Avoid moving or renumbering IDs after a record has been used.

Simple client record checklist

  • Client name and preferred written contact method.
  • Pet names and basic profiles linked to the correct client.
  • Current routine or preference notes that are useful during a normal visit.
  • Start date and active/inactive status.
  • A separate secure location for sensitive access, health, or emergency information.
Privacy reminder: collect only what your service needs. Protect personal information and follow your own business policies and applicable privacy requirements.
